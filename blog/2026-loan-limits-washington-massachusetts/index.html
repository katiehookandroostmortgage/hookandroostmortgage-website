2026 loan limits in Washington and Massachusetts, explained

Conforming, FHA and jumbo limits for the places I work, and what they mean for your purchase.

By Katie Luis-Hermina · November 17, 2026

Every year, the agencies behind most home loans update their loan limits. Those limits decide whether your loan counts as conforming, whether an FHA loan can cover the price you're looking at, and when you cross into jumbo territory. Here's what the 2026 numbers look like in the places I work.

Limits are set each year by the FHFA (conforming) and HUD (FHA). A loan above the conforming limit is generally a jumbo loan. Ask me for the exact limit for your county and property type.

What a conforming loan limit means for you

A conforming loan is a conventional loan that fits the size limits set by the Federal Housing Finance Agency. For 2026, the baseline limit for a single-family home is $832,750 in most counties. In higher-cost areas, the limit is higher: $1,063,750 in King, Pierce and Snohomish counties in Washington, and $962,550 in Suffolk, Middlesex, Norfolk, Essex and Plymouth counties in Massachusetts.

If your loan amount is above your county's limit, it's generally a jumbo loan. Jumbo loans aren't bad, they just have their own rules, which vary from lender to lender.

FHA limits work a little differently

HUD sets FHA loan limits by county too. The 2026 floor for a single-family home is $541,287, which applies in many Washington counties, including Spokane, and in Western Mass counties like Hampden. In the Seattle area, the FHA limit matches the conforming limit at $1,063,750. On Cape Cod, it's $828,000.

What about VA loans?

If you're a veteran or service member with full entitlement, the VA doesn't cap how much you can borrow. Your budget comes down to what you qualify for. If you have a VA loan on another home, your remaining entitlement may be tied to your county's conforming limit. Learn more about VA loans.

Multi-family homes have higher limits

Buying a duplex, triple-decker or fourplex and living in one unit? Limits for 2- to 4-unit properties are higher than for single-family homes, which can be a big help in places like Boston and Worcester.
